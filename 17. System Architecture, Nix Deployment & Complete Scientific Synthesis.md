## 1. Executive Summary & Clinical Mission

### 1.1 The Clinical Challenge: NDM-1 Carbapenemase
New Delhi metallo-$\beta$-lactamase-1 (NDM-1) is a B1 subclass zinc-dependent metallo-enzyme that hydrolyzes virtually all $\beta$-lactam antibiotics, including last-resort carbapenems (meropenem, imipenem, ertapenem), third-generation cephalosporins (cefotaxime), and penicillins (ampicillin).

Across hospitals globally, clinical strains cataloged in the **Beta-Lactamase Database (BLDB)** (NDM-1 through NDM-77) have acquired substitutions at **over 67 distinct residue positions**. When a novel variant emerges in an intensive care unit or clinical trial, clinicians and pharmaceutical chemists require certified guarantees on whether the mutation retains catalytic resistance or destabilizes the enzyme.

### 1.2 The Failure of Standard Machine Learning
Modern protein language models (ESM-2) and structural predictors (AlphaFold, standard Graph Neural Networks) output uncalibrated scalar point predictions $\hat{y}$. Heuristic confidence metrics (e.g., pLDDT) measure model self-consistency, not physical error distributions, and break down under non-conservative mutations, epistatic interactions, or novel allosteric shifts.

### 1.3 What Z-DynGNN Accomplishes
**Z-DynGNN** (*Z-score Calibrated Dynamics-Aware Graph Neural Network*) is an end-to-end framework pairing directional biophysical perturbation dynamics with **Distribution-Free Conformal Risk Control**. For any target miscoverage rate $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ (e.g., $\alpha = 0.05$ for 95% confidence), Z-DynGNN provides finite-sample certified prediction intervals:

$$\mathcal{C}(x_i) = \left[\hat{\mu}(x_i) - B_i, \; \hat{\mu}(x_i) + B_i\right]$$

with the mathematical guarantee that:

$$\mathbb{P}\left(y_i \in \mathcal{C}(x_i)\right) \ge 1 - \alpha$$

without assuming Gaussianity, linearity, or asymptotic sample sizes.

## 2. Theoretical Foundation: The "Correction v1" Audit & Solution

### 2.1 Technical Audit of the Original Strategy (Route A in Guide v7)
The initial project proposal attempted to establish generalization bounds for protein GNNs by combining the **Kim–Vu sandwich theorem** (Behague, Il’kovič, Montgomery, Oct 2025; Gao et al. 2020) with **Schudy–Sviridenko (2012) polynomial concentration** and PAC-Bayes $KL(Q \parallel P)$ bounds.

As proven in `01. Correction v1 - NDM-1 Strategy.md`, Route A was invalid due to four fundamental category errors:
1. **Deterministic Structure vs. Random Graph Ensembles:** Coupling is an equivalence between probability measures. The Kim–Vu sandwich theorem establishes couplings between random regular graphs $\mathcal{G}(n, d)$ and Erdős–Rényi ensembles $\mathcal{G}(n, p)$. NDM-1 is a static, deterministic physical crystal structure (PDB 3SPU) with fixed coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Sandwiching a deterministic protein graph between random graph ensembles is mathematically undefined.
2. **Asymptotic Fallacy ($d = \omega(\log n)$):** $\omega(\log n)$ is an asymptotic growth limit as $n \to \infty$. It cannot be evaluated on a single finite integer pair $(n=244, d=8)$. In finite-sample regimes, non-asymptotic constants and $o(1)$ coupling error probabilities are of order $\mathcal{O}(1)$ for small proteins.
3. **Severe Degree Heterogeneity (Violation of Near-Regularity):** Empirical contact degree analysis on PDB 3SPU demonstrates an 8.0 Å C$\alpha$ degree range of $\delta = 4$ (solvent-exposed loops) to $\Delta = 18$ (deep hydrophobic core). The heterogeneity spread $(\Delta - \delta)/\Delta = 77.78\%$ directly violates the near-regularity condition $\Delta - \delta \ll \Delta$.
4. **Multilinear Walks vs. Nonlinear GNN Expressivity:** Modeling GNN message passing as unweighted walk polynomials eliminates nonlinear activations ($\text{SiLU}$) and continuous radial basis functions. Furthermore, Schudy–Sviridenko constants scale as $(Ck)^k \gg 10^2$, yielding completely vacuous error bounds on normalized fitness ranges ($[-4, +1]$).
5. **PAC-Bayes Pointwise Category Error:** PAC-Bayesian theorems bound the population expectation $\mathbb{E}_{(x, y)}[\ell(f(x), y)]$ over model weight distributions; they cannot supply a deterministic per-residue bound $|y_i - \hat{y}_i| \le B_i$.

### 2.2 The Revised Framework: Topology-Conditioned & Cluster-Adjusted Conformal Risk Control
Correction v1 replaced the random-graph approach with distribution-free Conformal Prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates; Romano et al.):
* **Studentized Non-Conformity Scores:**
  $$R_i = \frac{|y_i - \hat{\mu}(x_i)|}{\max(\hat{\sigma}(x_i), \varepsilon)}$$
* **Finite-Sample Quantile Calculation:**
  For calibration sample size $m$:
  $$p = \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{\lceil (m + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{m}\right), \quad \hat{q} = \text{Quantile}\left(\{R_1, \dots, R_m\}, \; p\right)$$
* **Residue-Specific Confidence Bound:**
  $$B_i = \hat{q} \cdot \hat{\sigma}(x_i)$$
* **One-Sided Upper Conformal Bound for Clinical Safety:**
  To specifically protect against treatment failure (resistance overshoot) where $y_i > \hat{\mu}(x_i)$:
  $$R_i^{\text{upper}} = \frac{y_i - \hat{\mu}(x_i)}{\max(\hat{\sigma}(x_i), \varepsilon)}, \quad \mathcal{C}_{\text{upper}}(x_i) = \left(-\infty, \; \hat{\mu}(x_i) + \hat{q}_{\text{upper}} \cdot \hat{\sigma}(x_i)\right]$$

## 3. Biophysical Dynamics Engine (`src/zdyngnn/dynamics/`)

### 3.1 Structural Parsing & Contact Topology
* **PDB 3SPU:** Single-crystal X-ray structure of native NDM-1 at 1.30 Å resolution.
* **Residue Coordinate Extraction:** Chain A, 228 resolved residues spanning positions 43 through 270.
* **Contact Adjacency Matrix:** Binary contact graph with Euclidean distance threshold $d_0 = 8.0\text{ \AA}$ between C$\alpha$ atoms:
  $$A_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i \ne j \text{ and } \|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j\|_2 \le 8.0\text{ \AA} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
  Mean contact degree $\mu_d = 10.26$, minimum $\delta = 4$, maximum $\Delta = 18$.

### 3.2 Elastic Network Models (ENM)
* **Gaussian Network Model (GNM):**
  * Kirchhoff Laplacian matrix: $\Gamma = \text{diag}(\mathbf{d}) - A$.
  * Spectral decomposition: $\Gamma = \sum_{k=1}^N \lambda_k \mathbf{v}_k \mathbf{v}_k^T$.
  * Fiedler eigenvalue (algebraic connectivity): $\lambda_2 = 0.2832$.
  * Pseudo-inverse covariance: $\Gamma^\dagger = \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{1}{\lambda_k} \mathbf{v}_k \mathbf{v}_k^T$.
  * Theoretical mean-square fluctuations (MSF) $\langle (\Delta r_i)^2 \rangle \propto \Gamma^\dagger_{ii}$ correlate strongly with experimental crystallographic B-factors: **Pearson $r = 0.7077$**.
* **Anisotropic Network Model (ANM):**
  * $3N \times 3N$ Hessian matrix $H$ modeling directional spring constants $\gamma = 1.0$ along contacts.
  * Rigid-body translations and rotations (6 zero modes) removed via Moore–Penrose pseudo-inversion $H^\dagger$.

### 3.3 Perturbation Response Scanning (PRS) & Directional Coupling ($\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$)
* **Response Matrix $S_{ij}$:** RMS displacement amplitude of residue $i$ under isotropic unit force perturbation on residue $j$:
  $$S_{ij} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3} \text{Tr}\left(H^\dagger_{ij} (H^\dagger_{ij})^T\right)}$$
* **Dynamic Coupling Index (DCI):**
  $$\text{DCI}_{ij} = \frac{S_{ij}}{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N S_{ik}}$$
* **Asymmetric Dynamic Coupling Index ($\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$):**
  $$\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, j) = \text{DCI}_{ij} - \text{DCI}_{ji}$$
  * Strictly anti-symmetric: $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}^T = -\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$.
  * Zero diagonal: $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, i) = 0$.
  * Biophysical interpretation: $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, j) > 0$ identifies residue $j$ as an allosteric driver exerting directional dynamical control over residue $i$.

## 4. Z-DynGNN Model Architecture & Training Invariants

### 4.1 Neural Network Architecture
* **Node Representations ($N = 228, D = 34$):**
  * Residue wild-type amino acid identity: 20-dimensional one-hot vector.
  * Residue biophysical dynamics features (14 dimensions): contact degree, GNM MSF, ANM MSF, B-factors, local clustering coefficient, Laplacian eigenvector coordinates ($\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3$), and aggregate PRS driver scores.
* **Continuous Radial Basis Edge Distances:**
  16 Gaussian RBF centers spanning $0.0$ to $8.0\text{ \AA}$:
  $$\phi_k(d_{ij}) = \exp\left(-\gamma (d_{ij} - \mu_k)^2\right)$$
* **Directional Edge Dynamics Injection:**
  Message-passing layers concatenate source representations, RBF edge features, and the directional $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(j \to i)$ scalar:
  $$\mathbf{m}_{j \to i} = \text{MLP}_{\text{msg}}\left([\mathbf{h}_j, \; \text{RBF}(d_{ij}), \; \text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(j, i)]\right)$$
  $$\mathbf{h}_i^{(t+1)} = \text{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{h}_i^{(t)} + \text{MLP}_{\text{update}}\left([\mathbf{h}_i^{(t)}, \; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \mathbf{m}_{j \to i}]\right)\right)$$
* **Dual Heteroscedastic Mutation Heads:**
  Given a mutation at position $p$ with WT amino acid $a_{\text{wt}}$ and mutant amino acid $a_{\text{mut}}$:
  $$\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}} = [\mathbf{h}_p, \; \text{OneHot}(a_{\text{wt}}), \; \text{OneHot}(a_{\text{mut}})] \in \mathbb{R}^{64 + 20 + 20}$$
  $$\hat{\mu} = \text{MLP}_{\mu}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}}), \quad \log \hat{\sigma}^2 = \text{clamp}\left(\text{MLP}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}}), \; -10.0, \; 5.0\right), \quad \hat{\sigma} = \exp\left(0.5 \log \hat{\sigma}^2\right)$$

### 4.2 Strict Position-Disjoint Partitioning (70% / 15% / 15%)
To prevent spatial memorization, residues are partitioned disjointly:
* **Train Positions:** 163 residues (3,062 missense variants)
* **Calibration Positions:** 32 residues (597 missense variants)
* **Test Positions:** 33 residues (612 missense variants)
* Invariant: $\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{calib}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{calib}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$.

## 5. Conformal Risk Control & Cluster Calibration

### 5.1 Root Cause of Stratification Underperformance
Under position-disjoint splits, distributing 32 calibration positions across 11 structural strata resulted in an average of only 2.9 positions per stratum. Several strata contained 0–2 positions, meaning all 19–38 calibration mutations in that stratum originated from the exact same residues. Idiosyncratic residue noise caused stratum quantiles to miscalibrate on held-out test residues (e.g., Stratum 3 at 73.7% coverage).

### 5.2 Cluster-Adjusted Conformal Solution
In deep mutational scanning, mutations at the same residue position share structural correlation. Therefore, the **residue position is the true physical unit of exchangeability**.
* Within each calibration position $k$, compute the $q_{\text{agg}} = 0.90$ percentile of studentized residuals:
  $$R_{\text{pos}}(k) = \text{Quantile}\left(\{R_{k, m}\}; \; 0.90\right)$$
* Compute the conformal quantile over the position-level scores:
  $$\hat{q} = \text{Quantile}\left(\{R_{\text{pos}}(1), \dots, R_{\text{pos}}(32)\}, \; \frac{\lceil(32 + 1)(1 - \alpha)\rceil}{32}\right)$$
* Apply test intervals:
  $$B_i = \hat{q} \cdot \hat{\sigma}(x_i)$$

### 6.4 Critical Audit Findings & Resolutions (Review v2 & v3)
1. **Decision Ambiguity under Two-Sided Bounds:** Because certified two-sided intervals have widths of 8.6 to 15.5 log2-units, almost every variant spans the resistance breakpoint ($y \ge -2.0$ vs $y < -5.0$). This results in virtually 100% of calls being classified as *"Ambiguous (Triage for Lab)"*.
2. **Point Estimate Bias on M154L:** $\hat{\mu}$ predicts -3.17 to -5.86 (deleterious) while true lab fitness is slightly positive (+0.03 to +0.49). Containment succeeds because the interval is wide, not because the point estimate is accurate on this specific allosteric mutation.
3. **Meropenem Discrepancy on M248L:** M248L true fitness is $+1.22$, whereas the upper bound is $+1.14$ (a miscoverage of 0.08 units). This represents the single miss in the 22-variant set, fully consistent with the 95% nominal confidence rate (21/22 = 95.5%).
4. **Actionable Resolution: One-Sided Upper Conformal Bounds:**
   * Switching clinical risk triage to the one-sided upper bound shrinks the error margin to **3.29–4.71 log2-units**.
   * Focuses directly on preventing clinical treatment failure (resistance overshoot).
   * Achieves **99.0% to 100.0% empirical upper coverage** across all three antibiotics.
