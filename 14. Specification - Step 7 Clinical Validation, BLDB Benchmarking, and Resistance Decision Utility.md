## 2. Clinical Benchmark 1: Beta-Lactamase Database (BLDB) Hospital Variants

### 2.1 The Biological Target
The **Beta-Lactamase Database (BLDB)** is the global standard repository cataloging all clinically isolated $\beta$-lactamases recovered from infected patients in hospitals.

As of 2025–2026, **69 natural clinical variants of NDM (NDM-1 through NDM-69)** have been identified worldwide. These variants carry single or multiple substitutions across **67 distinct positions** in the protein sequence:
* Examples of prevalent hospital substitutions:
  * **M154L** (NDM-4, NDM-5, NDM-7, NDM-8, NDM-12, NDM-13, NDM-14, NDM-15, NDM-16)
  * **D130N** (NDM-19)
  * **D95N** (NDM-24)
  * **A233V** (NDM-3)
  * **Q211K** (NDM-9)
  * **K211N / C208L** (catalytic pocket shifts)

### 2.2 Validation Protocol
For every single amino acid substitution present in the BLDB catalog that is covered in the deep mutational scanning landscape:
1. Extract Z-DynGNN's predicted mean fitness $\hat{\mu}$ and aleatoric scale $\hat{\sigma}$.
2. Look up the Step 6 production certified confidence interval:
   $$\mathcal{C}(x_{\text{BLDB}}) = \left[\hat{\mu} - B_i, \; \hat{\mu} + B_i\right]$$
3. Verify that:
   * **Containment Rate:** True experimental fitness $y_{\text{lab}} \in \mathcal{C}(x_{\text{BLDB}})$.
   * **Phenotypic Integrity:** The lower bound $L_i = \hat{\mu} - B_i$ certifies that the variant retains functional carbapenemase fitness (non-deleterious).

## 3. Clinical Benchmark 2: Deep Dive on the Prevalent M154L Super-Variant

### 3.1 Why M154L Is the Primary Target
**M154L** is the most successful allosteric mutation in the clinical evolution of NDM-1. It does not touch the active-site zinc ions directly; instead, it resides on the solvent-exposed $\alpha3$ helix.

Biophysical mechanisms demonstrated in recent literature (Huang et al. 2025, *JACS*):
* Under physiological zinc starvation (e.g., in human urinary tract or bloodstream during immune response), wild-type NDM-1 loses a zinc ion and unfolds.
* M154L rigidifies the allosteric $\alpha3\text{--}L8\text{--}\beta8$ network, increasing zinc affinity and stabilizing the enzyme against host metal-chelating defenses.

### 3.2 Model Evaluation Targets
1. **Predicted Point Fitness:** $\hat{\mu}(\text{M154L})$ across Ampicillin, Cefotaxime, and Meropenem.
2. **Predicted Scale:** $\hat{\sigma}(\text{M154L})$ reflecting local conformational coupling.
3. **Certified Bounds:** Verify that $[\hat{\mu} - B_i, \hat{\mu} + B_i]$ tightly encapsulates the observed clinical resistance score without over-expanding.
4. **Allosteric Coupling Matrix:** Cross-reference $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(154, \text{active\_site})$ against catalytic residues His120, His122, Asp124, His189, Cys208, and His250.

## 4. Clinical Benchmark 3: Certified Decision Making via Resistance Breakpoints

In clinical microbiology and hospital antimicrobial stewardship, physicians do not make decisions on continuous numbers—they make binary or ternary decisions:
$$\text{Is the bacterial infection Resistant (treatment failure) or Susceptible (drug works)?}$$

### 4.1 Categorical Mapping from Continuous Fitness
Based on minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) dilution breakpoints and wild-type growth thresholds:
* **Resistant (Active Enzyme):** Experimental fitness $y \ge -2.0$ log2-units (bacterial growth unimpaired at high antibiotic concentrations).
* **Intermediate / Reduced Fitness:** $-5.0 \le y < -2.0$ log2-units.
* **Susceptible / Inactive (Deleterious):** $y < -5.0$ log2-units (enzyme inactivated; bacterial clearance achieved).

### 4.2 Three-State Certified Decision Framework
By utilizing both the lower bound $L_i = \hat{\mu}_i - B_i$ and upper bound $U_i = \hat{\mu}_i + B_i$, Z-DynGNN produces **certified clinical calls**:

1. **Certified Resistant:**
   $$L_i = \hat{\mu}_i - B_i \ge -2.0$$
   * *Clinical Interpretation:* With 95% mathematical certainty, the bacterium is resistant. The antibiotic is contraindicated.
2. **Certified Susceptible:**
   $$U_i = \hat{\mu}_i + B_i < -5.0$$
   * *Clinical Interpretation:* With 95% mathematical certainty, the mutation destroys enzyme activity. The antibiotic is effective.
3. **Ambiguous / Flagged for Wet-Lab Triage:**
   $$\text{Interval } [L_i, U_i] \text{ spans the threshold}$$
   * *Clinical Interpretation:* The AI cannot certify the phenotype with 95% certainty. The variant is automatically flagged for emergency wet-lab microbiological testing before clinical deployment.

### 4.3 Decision Utility Metrics Evaluated
* **Certified Sensitivity (True Resistant Rate):** Fraction of true resistant variants certified as resistant.
* **Certified Specificity (True Inactive Rate):** Fraction of true inactive variants certified as susceptible.
* **False Safety Rate (Critical Error):** True resistant variants incorrectly certified as susceptible (target: $0.0\%$).
* **Triage Efficiency:** Fraction of total mutations where AI makes a decisive call without requiring wet-lab assaying.
