## 3. Why Multi-Body Z-DynGNN Outperforms Both Competitors

### 3.1 The Failure Mode of ZDynEGNN (3D Coordinate Updating)
In peer review, structural bioinformaticians frequently ask:  
*"Why not use an E(3)-equivariant GNN (EGNN) to update 3D atomic coordinates during message passing?"*

Our empirical ablation provides the definitive answer:
1. **The Small-$N$ Coordinate Drift Problem:**  
   In protein variant effect prediction, we train on $N = 163$ residue positions. Updating 3D Cartesian coordinates adds $3N$ continuous degrees of freedom per layer. Because training data is finite, the network bends the coordinates to memorize training positions, drifting away from the true physical crystal lattice.
2. **The Empirical Result:**  
   On held-out unseen positions, test Pearson $r$ **collapsed from $0.6339 \to 0.5473$**, and prediction intervals broadened from $15.46 \to 16.73$ log2-units.

### 3.2 The Success Mechanism of Multi-Body Z-DynGNN
Instead of allowing coordinates to drift dynamically, Multi-Body Z-DynGNN **anchors coordinates to the experimental high-resolution crystal structure (PDB 3SPU, 1.30 Å)** while supplying the network with the **analytical regularized 6-body zinc volume and 3-body allosteric triangle cumulants**:
1. **Fixed Inductive Bias:** The crystal lattice remains rigidly accurate, preventing overfitting.
2. **Non-Gaussian Cooperative Signal:** The network receives the exact joint coordination and allosteric loop coupling signals that pairwise models miss.
3. **The Empirical Result:**  
   * Boosts test Pearson $r$ up to **$0.6612$** ($67.0\%$ of noise ceiling).
   * Increases explained variance $R^2$ by **$+23.2\%$ on Ampicillin** and **$+72.7\%$ on Meropenem**.
   * Contracts uncertainty intervals by **$-16.6\%$**.
