## 2. Mathematical Proof of Resolving the Triage Deadlock

### 2.1 Why the Symmetric Two-Sided Interval Failed
In earlier versions, decisions were mapped using a symmetric interval $\mathcal{C}(x_i) = [\hat{\mu} - B, \; \hat{\mu} + B]$:
* $\text{Certified Resistant} \iff \hat{\mu} - B \ge -2.0 \iff \hat{\mu} \ge -2.0 + B$
* $\text{Certified Susceptible} \iff \hat{\mu} + B < -5.0 \iff \hat{\mu} < -5.0 - B$

Because $B \approx 4.5\text{ to }7.4$ log2-units:
* To be Certified Resistant required $\hat{\mu} \ge \mathbf{+5.4}$ (*impossible; max biological fitness is $+1.5$*).
* To be Certified Susceptible required $\hat{\mu} < \mathbf{-12.4}$ (*impossible; floor is $-6.5$*).
* **Result:** 100% of mutations fell into the dead zone between $[-12.4, +5.4]$, yielding 0% decisive calls.

### 2.2 How Decoupled Dual Testing Unlocks Decisiveness
Instead of requiring one interval to span the entire biological space, Step 12 formulates two separate directional questions:
1. **Directional Safety Test:** Can we guarantee fitness is below a threshold?
   $$U_{\text{upper}}(x_i) < \tau \implies \mathbf{\text{Decisively Certified Safe / Sub-Threshold}}$$
2. **Directional Activity Test:** Can we guarantee fitness is above a threshold?
   $$L_{\text{lower}}(x_i) \ge \tau \implies \mathbf{\text{Decisively Certified Active / Above-Threshold}}$$
