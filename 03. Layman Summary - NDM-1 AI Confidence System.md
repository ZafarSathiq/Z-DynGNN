## 1. The Real-World Crisis: Antibiotic-Resistant Superbugs

Bacteria are constantly mutating to survive modern medicine. 

One of the world's most dangerous "superbug" weapons is an enzyme called **NDM-1** (New Delhi Metallo-$\beta$-lactamase-1). When bacteria produce NDM-1, they destroy our last-line emergency antibiotics (**carbapenems**)—drugs doctors only prescribe when everything else has failed.

Infections with NDM-producing bacteria have surged globally. Worse, NDM-1 is not static:
* Doctors have already cataloged **69 different clinical strains** (NDM-1 through NDM-69) in hospitals worldwide.
* Mutations have appeared at **67 distinct positions** across the protein.
* Every time a new mutation emerges, doctors and pharmaceutical chemists must urgently know:
  1. *Will this new strain be resistant to our newest backup drugs?*
  2. *Will an experimental drug currently being developed still work, or has the mutation ruined the target?*

## 2. The Hidden Danger in Today's AI Models

Modern AI models (like AlphaFold and ESM-2) have revolutionized biology by predicting protein structures and mutations faster than wet labs can test them. 

However, they suffer from a dangerous flaw: **blind overconfidence**.

* **No Certified Safety Margins:** Current AI outputs a single point number (e.g., *"fitness score = -1.2"*). It does not tell scientists how far off that number could realistically be.
* **Heuristic Scores Are Unreliable:** Built-in confidence scores (like AlphaFold's pLDDT) measure how confident the model *feels*, but they frequently break down when a protein mutates into something new.
* **The Real-World Cost:** If an AI is silently wrong about a mutation:
  * A pharmaceutical company can spend **3 years and $50 million** synthesizing a drug against a false target that completely fails in clinical trials.
  * A hospital can fail to isolate a patient carrying a deadly, newly evolved variant because an AI tool incorrectly classified it as harmless.

## 4. What Was Broken in the Original Math (And How We Fixed It)

### The Original Flaw in Guide v7:
The original proposal attempted to prove safety using a mathematical formula called the *Kim–Vu sandwich theorem*. 
* **The Analogy:** That theorem was designed for calculating probabilities of random, evenly spaced networks of infinite size (like counting random coin flips across millions of nodes).
* **The Reality:** A real protein is a physical 3D object made of 244 atoms arranged in tight folds. Some parts are deeply buried and tightly packed; other parts are floppy surface loops. Using the Kim–Vu theorem on a single protein was like using a blueprint for an infinite grid of city streets to navigate a mountainous hiking trail—it didn't fit reality, and the error bounds exploded to infinity (became vacuous).

### The Fix in Correction v1:
We replaced the unworkable theory with a proven, rigorous framework called **Topology-Conditioned Conformal Prediction**:
* **How it works:** We take a portion of known mutations that the AI was never allowed to see during training. We measure exactly how wrong the AI tends to be in different parts of the protein (core vs. surface loops vs. flexible hinges).
* **The Result:** We calculate an exact, finite-sample statistical shield around every prediction. The bounds are guaranteed to cover reality 95% of the time, and the safety margins are narrow enough ($\pm 0.35$) to be genuinely useful for drug designers.
