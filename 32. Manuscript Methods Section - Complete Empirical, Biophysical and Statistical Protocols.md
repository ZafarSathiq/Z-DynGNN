## 1. Structural Coordinate Ingestion & Protein Graph Construction

### 1.1 Crystal Structures and Coordinate Alignment
The primary structural framework of New Delhi metallo-$\beta$-lactamase-1 (NDM-1) was constructed using the high-resolution single-crystal X-ray diffraction structure from the Protein Data Bank (PDB accession **`3SPU`**, 1.30 Å resolution). Coordinates were restricted to Chain A, spanning residues 43 to 270 (228 resolved amino acids). Residues 1 to 42 comprise the cleaved N-terminal signal peptide and hydrophobic lipobox anchor, which are absent in active soluble enzyme crystals.

To conduct multi-conformational structural comparative audits (Step 11), four additional high-resolution crystal structures were parsed and aligned:
1. **`PDB 4HL2`** (1.05 Å resolution, Chain A, residues 43–270): Native NDM-1 complexed with hydrolyzed Ampicillin.
2. **`PDB 4RL0`** (1.30 Å resolution, Chain B, residues 43–270): Native NDM-1 complexed with hydrolyzed Meropenem intermediate.
3. **`PDB 5ZGY`** (0.95 Å resolution, Chain A, residues 43–270): Sub-angstrom resolution structure resolved at pH 7.5.
4. **`PDB 5YD7`** (1.70 Å resolution, Chain A, residues 32–262): Crystal structure of VIM-2 metallo-$\beta$-lactamase (homologous Class B reference).

### 1.2 Contact Graph Topology
For each crystal structure, a spatial interaction graph $G = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ was constructed over $N = 228$ residue nodes corresponding to $\text{C}\alpha$ atomic coordinates $\mathbf{r}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$. Edge connections between distinct residues $i \ne j$ were established based on an 8.0 Å Euclidean distance cutoff:
$$A_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i \ne j \quad \text{and} \quad \|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j\|_2 \le 8.0\text{ \AA} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
The resulting contact graph has mean degree $\mu_d = 10.26$, minimum degree $\delta = 4$ (solvent-exposed loop tips), maximum degree $\Delta = 18$ (buried core), and degree spread $(\Delta - \delta)/\Delta = 77.78\%$.

## 2. Biophysical Dynamics Engine: Elastic Network Models & PRS

### 2.1 Gaussian Network Model (GNM)
Equilibrium thermodynamic fluctuations were modeled via the Gaussian Network Model. The $N \times N$ Kirchhoff (Laplacian) connectivity matrix $\Gamma$ was defined as:
$$\Gamma = \mathbf{D} - A, \quad \Gamma_{ii} = d_i = \sum_{j \ne i} A_{ij}, \quad \Gamma_{ij} = -A_{ij} \ (i \ne j)$$
Spectral decomposition of $\Gamma$ yields eigenvalues $0 = \lambda_1 < \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_N$ and orthogonal eigenvectors $\mathbf{v}_k$. The second eigenvalue $\lambda_2 = 0.2832$ represents the algebraic connectivity (Fiedler eigenvalue) of the fold. The Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse covariance matrix omitting the rigid-body translational zero mode ($k=1$) was evaluated as:
$$\Gamma^\dagger = \sum_{k=2}^N \frac{1}{\lambda_k} \mathbf{v}_k \mathbf{v}_k^T$$
Theoretical mean-square fluctuations (MSF) $\langle (\Delta r_i)^2 \rangle = \frac{3 k_B T}{\gamma} \Gamma^\dagger_{ii}$ were cross-validated against experimental crystallographic B-factors extracted from PDB 3SPU ($B_i = \frac{8\pi^2}{3}\langle (\Delta r_i)^2 \rangle$), yielding a strong physical correlation of **Pearson $r = 0.7077$**.

### 2.2 Anisotropic Network Model (ANM)
Directional 3D structural dynamics were computed using the Anisotropic Network Model. The $3N \times 3N$ Cartesian Hessian matrix $H$ was constructed from $3 \times 3$ inter-residue submatrices:
$$H_{ij}^{3 \times 3} = -\frac{\gamma}{\|\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j\|^2} (\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j)(\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_j)^T \quad (i \ne j, A_{ij} = 1)$$
$$H_{ii}^{3 \times 3} = -\sum_{j \ne i} H_{ij}^{3 \times 3}$$
with spring constant $\gamma = 1.0\text{ kcal}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{\AA}^2)$. The 6 rigid-body translational and rotational zero modes were eliminated via Moore–Penrose pseudo-inversion:
$$H^\dagger = \sum_{k=7}^{3N} \frac{1}{\lambda_k} \mathbf{u}_k \mathbf{u}_k^T$$

### 2.3 Perturbation Response Scanning (PRS) & $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$
To simulate conformational communication pathways, Perturbation Response Scanning (PRS) was performed by applying isotropic unit force kicks ($\|\mathbf{F}_j\| = 1$) to each residue $j$. The root-mean-square displacement amplitude experienced by residue $i$ was computed as:
$$S_{ij} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3} \text{Tr}\left( H_{ij}^\dagger (H_{ij}^\dagger)^T \right)}$$
The row-normalized Dynamic Coupling Index ($\text{DCI}_{ij}$) and the strictly antisymmetric Asymmetric Dynamic Coupling Index ($\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$) were derived as:
$$\text{DCI}_{ij} = \frac{S_{ij}}{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N S_{ik}}, \quad \text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, j) = \text{DCI}_{ij} - \text{DCI}_{ji}$$
Properties: $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}^T = -\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}$, with $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, i) = 0$. Residues with $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(i, j) > 0$ act as allosteric drivers exerting directional mechanical control over target residue $i$.

## 3. Deep Mutational Scanning Ingestion & Noise Ceiling Statistics

### 3.1 DMS Landscape
High-throughput mutational fitness data was ingested from Chen et al. 2024 (*Nature Communications*, Supplementary Data 3). The dataset contains 4,271 missense substitutions measured across 9 distinct drug concentrations (38,439 total measurements):
* **Ampicillin:** 32.0, 256.0, 512.0 µg/mL
* **Cefotaxime:** 2.0, 16.0, 32.0 µg/mL
* **Meropenem:** 0.0625, 0.25, 0.5 µg/mL

### 3.2 Law of Total Variance & Physical Noise Ceilings
To establish the physical upper limit of model performance under irreducible wet-lab experimental noise, the total biological variance was decomposed according to the Law of Total Variance (Hsu et al. 2025 *PNAS*):
$$\text{Var}(y) = \text{Var}(f_{\text{biological}}) + \mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2]$$
where $\mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2] = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N s_{i, \text{repl}}^2$ is the mean experimental replicate variance. The theoretical maximum achievable determination ($R^2_{\max}$) and Pearson correlation ($r_{\max}$) were defined as:
$$R^2_{\max} = 1 - \frac{\mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2]}{\text{Var}(y)}, \quad r_{\max} = \sqrt{R^2_{\max}}, \quad \text{SNR} = \frac{\text{Var}(y) - \mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2]}{\mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2]}$$
For Ampicillin ($256.0\,\mu\text{g/mL}$): $\text{Var}(y) = 9.7533, \mathbb{E}[\sigma_\epsilon^2] = 0.2638 \implies R^2_{\max} = 0.9729, r_{\max} = \mathbf{0.9864}$ ($\text{SNR} = 35.97$). Normalized model metrics were reported as $\text{Norm } r = r_{\text{model}} / r_{\max}$ and $\text{Norm } R^2 = R^2_{\text{model}} / R^2_{\max}$.

## 4. Strict Spatial Partitioning & Zero Data Leakage Protocols

### 4.1 Position-Disjoint Partitioning (70% / 15% / 15%)
To prevent spatial memorization, all 228 residue positions were partitioned into three mutually disjoint subsets using stratified degree/dynamics sampling (seed = 42):
* **Training Positions ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}}$):** 163 positions (3,062 missense mutations, 71.7%)
* **Calibration Positions ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{calib}}$):** 32 positions (597 missense mutations, 14.0%)
* **Test Positions ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}$):** 33 positions (612 missense mutations, 14.3%)
* **Invariants:** $\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{calib}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{calib}} \cap \mathcal{P}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$. Held-out test positions were never exposed during model training or conformal quantile calibration.

### 4.2 Clinical Validation 3-Way Partition Safeguard
When validating against the global catalog of natural hospital strains from the Beta-Lactamase Database (BLDB, NDM-1 through NDM-77), every variant was routed into three strictly isolated buckets:
1. **Bucket 1 (`TEST`, $n=7$):** Hospital mutations located at test positions (`A74T`, `G200R`, `E170K`, `D223Y`, `K242Q`, `P185S`, `H122Q`). Evaluated as the pure generalization benchmark.
2. **Bucket 2 (`CALIB`, $n=11$):** Hospital mutations located at calibration positions (`M154L`, `G69S`, `M154V`, `M248L`, `A55S`, `D267N`, `Q151L`, `P179R`, `A252D`, `A99T`, `G206A`). Evaluated for calibration consistency.
3. **Bucket 3 (`TRAIN_CONTROL`, $n=30$):** Hospital mutations located at training positions (`D95N`, `V88L`, `A233V`, `D130N`, `D130G`, `E152K`, `G222D`, `I101L`, `G222S`, `A266V`, etc.). Strictly segregated into internal consistency controls; **never evaluated in test generalization statistics**.

## 5. Z-DynGNN Architecture & Model Training

### 5.1 Node and Edge Representations
* **Node Features ($\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{34}$):** Concatenation of a 20-dimensional one-hot wild-type amino acid vector and a 14-dimensional biophysical dynamics vector (normalized degree $d_i$, clustering coefficient $C_i$, theoretical GNM MSF, experimental B-factors, PRS driver score, PRS sensor score, and 8 normalized Laplacian eigenvectors).
* **Continuous Edge Encodings ($\phi(d_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$):** Pairwise Euclidean distances expanded across 16 Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) centers spanning $0.0$ to $8.0\text{ \AA}$:
  $$\phi_k(d_{ij}) = \exp\left( -\gamma (d_{ij} - \mu_k)^2 \right), \quad \gamma = \frac{0.5}{(\Delta \mu)^2}$$

### 5.2 Directional Dynamics Message Passing
For each message-passing layer $t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, messages incorporate source node state, edge distance embeddings, and directional $\text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(j, i)$:
$$\mathbf{m}_{j \to i} = \text{MLP}_{\text{msg}}\left( \left[ \mathbf{h}_j^{(t)}, \; \text{Linear}(\phi(d_{ji})), \; \text{DCI}_{\text{asym}}(j, i) \right] \right)$$
$$\mathbf{h}_i^{(t+1)} = \text{LayerNorm}\left( \mathbf{h}_i^{(t)} + \text{MLP}_{\text{update}}\left( \left[ \mathbf{h}_i^{(t)}, \; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \mathbf{m}_{j \to i} \right] \right) \right)$$
with hidden dimension $d_h = 64$ and $\text{SiLU}$ non-linear activations.

### 5.3 Heteroscedastic Dual Heads & Loss Optimization
Given a single substitution $a_{\text{wt}} \to a_{\text{mut}}$ at residue position $p$:
$$\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}} = \left[ \mathbf{h}_p^{(3)}, \; \text{OneHot}(a_{\text{wt}}), \; \text{OneHot}(a_{\text{mut}}) \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{104}$$
$$\hat{\mu}(x) = \text{MLP}_{\mu}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}}), \quad \hat{\sigma}(x) = \exp\left( 0.5 \cdot \text{clamp}(\text{MLP}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{z}_{\text{mut}}), -10.0, 5.0) \right)$$
Training was performed exclusively on the 163 training positions using Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(y, \hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma}) = \frac{1}{2} \log(2\pi \hat{\sigma}^2) + \frac{(y - \hat{\mu})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}$$
using AdamW optimizer ($\text{lr} = 2 \times 10^{-3}$, weight decay $= 10^{-4}$, batch size $= 128$, epochs $= 35$, early stopping patience $= 8$).

## 6. Distribution-Free Conformal Risk Control & Calibration Engine

### 6.1 Cluster-Adjusted Conformal Calibration
To preserve finite-sample exchangeability under position-disjoint partitioning, non-conformity scores were computed using normalized studentized absolute residuals:
$$R_{k, j} = \frac{|y_{k, j} - \hat{\mu}_{k, j}|}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_{k, j}, \; \varepsilon)}$$
Within each of the $m = 32$ calibration positions, residuals were aggregated at percentile $q_{\text{agg}} = 0.90$:
$$R_{\text{pos}}(k) = \text{Quantile}\left( \{R_{k, j}\} ; \; 0.90 \right)$$
The production conformal quantile $\hat{q}_{\text{cluster}}$ was determined at the finite-sample adjusted level:
$$p = \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{\lceil (m + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{m}\right) = \frac{32}{32} = 1.0000$$
$$\hat{q}_{\text{cluster}} = \text{Quantile}\left( \{R_{\text{pos}}(1), \dots, R_{\text{pos}}(32)\}, \; p \right)$$
Prediction intervals on unseen positions were constructed as:
$$\mathcal{C}(x_i) = \left[ \hat{\mu}(x_i) - \hat{q}_{\text{cluster}} \hat{\sigma}(x_i), \; \hat{\mu}(x_i) + \hat{q}_{\text{cluster}} \hat{\sigma}(x_i) \right]$$
guaranteeing marginal finite-sample coverage $\mathbb{P}(y_i \in \mathcal{C}(x_i)) \ge 1 - \alpha$.

### 6.2 One-Sided Upper Conformal Bounds
To control clinical treatment failure (resistance overshoot), signed upper non-conformity scores were defined:
$$R_{k, j}^{\text{upper}} = \frac{y_{k, j} - \hat{\mu}_{k, j}}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_{k, j}, \; \varepsilon)}$$
Aggregating within positions ($q_{\text{agg}} = 0.90$) yields upper quantiles $\hat{q}_{\text{up}}$. Certified upper bounds:
$$U_{\text{upper}}(x_i) = \hat{\mu}(x_i) + \hat{q}_{\text{up}} \cdot \hat{\sigma}(x_i), \quad \mathbb{P}\left( y_i \le U_{\text{upper}}(x_i) \right) \ge 1 - \alpha$$
contracting certified margins by **58.0% to 68.1%** ($B_{\text{up}} \approx 3.3\text{--}4.7$ log2-units).

### 6.3 Winkler Proper Interval Scoring Rule
Prediction intervals were evaluated using the Winkler proper scoring rule:
$$\text{Score}_{\alpha}(L_i, U_i, y_i) = (U_i - L_i) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(L_i - y_i)\mathbb{I}(y_i < L_i) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(y_i - U_i)\mathbb{I}(y_i > U_i)$$

## 7. Decoupled Dual One-Sided Hypothesis Testing Framework (Step 12)

Clinical decision-making was decoupled into two independent directional tests:
1. **Upper Safety Screening (Hypothesis $H_0: y_i \ge \tau$):**
   $$U_{\text{upper}}(x_i) < \tau \implies \mathbf{\text{Decisively Certified Safe / Susceptible}} \quad (\ge 95\% \text{ confidence})$$
2. **Lower Activity Certification (Hypothesis $H_0: y_i < \tau$):**
   $$L_{\text{lower}}(x_i) = \hat{\mu}(x_i) - \hat{q}_{\text{low}} \hat{\sigma}(x_i) \ge \tau \implies \mathbf{\text{Decisively Certified Active / Resistant}} \quad (\ge 95\% \text{ confidence})$$

Thresholds:
* $\tau_{\text{wt}} = 0.0$ (Wild-type reference level)
* $\tau_{\text{res}} = -2.0$ (Clinical resistance breakpoint)
* $\tau_{\text{dead}} = -5.0$ (Assay inactivation floor)

## 8. Wet-Lab Physical $\text{EC}_{50}$ Microdilution & Combinatorial Epistasis (Step 9)

Physical microdilution assays were extracted from Chen et al. Supplementary Data 5. Quantitative $\text{EC}_{50}$ fold-changes in $\mu\text{g/mL}$ were log2-transformed: $y_{\text{phys}} = \log_2(\text{EC}_{50} / \text{EC}_{50}^{\text{WT}})$.

For double and triple mutants ($x_{AB}$), combinatorial predictions were accumulated across constituent mutations under zero retraining:
$$\hat{\mu}_{AB} = \sum_i \hat{\mu}_i, \quad \hat{\sigma}_{AB} = \sqrt{\sum_i \hat{\sigma}_i^2}, \quad B_{AB} = \hat{q} \hat{\sigma}_{AB}$$
$$\mathcal{C}(x_{AB}) = \left[ \hat{\mu}_{AB} - B_{AB}, \; \hat{\mu}_{AB} + B_{AB} \right]$$
Physical epistatic deviation was evaluated as:
$$\epsilon = y_{\text{observed}} - y_{\text{expected\_additive}}$$
classifying events into Synergistic Buffering ($\epsilon > 0.5$, e.g. `epi3 K211Y+Y229W`, $\epsilon = +1.87$) and Deleterious Interference ($\epsilon < -0.5$, e.g. `epi5 A204L+I259L`, $\epsilon = -1.10$).
